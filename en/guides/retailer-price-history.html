How do I compare price history across retailers?

Price history first describes one identifiable listing over time. A cross-retailer comparison is meaningful only when style number, version, market, and currency are explicit.

Content reviewed: 2026-09-08
Short answer

GearDrop shows price history for tracked products when data is available, and the iPhone app supports saved products, history, and price alerts. Before comparing retailers, verify the style number or SKU, gender, color, and version; then retain each retailer's original-currency observations and timestamps instead of automatically merging similar names.

Questions and answers

Can matching product names be merged across retailers?

No. Verify style number, version, gender, color, and market first. Keep records separate when identity cannot be established.

Does an observed low mean I should buy now?

No. It only describes the available observation window. Also check sizes, sale terms, landed cost, and return policy.

Why does a product have no price history?

Tracking may be new, the history may be too short, the source may have expired, or the product identity may not match reliably. Missing history does not mean there is no discount.

Three different price signals

Current price
The latest successful observation, not a checkout guarantee
Price history
Observed values for the same identifiable record at multiple times
Observed low
The lowest value in the available window, not an all-market or permanent low
A shorter window and fewer data points support a narrower conclusion.

Cross-retailer comparison steps

  1. Verify product identity
    Prefer an official style number or SKU, then check season, gender, color, size, and imagery.
  2. Keep original currencies
    Read each retailer's current price and history in its original currency before converting anything.
  3. Retain time and source
    Keep the retailer, original URL, and observation time with every comparison point.
  4. Calculate landed cost
    After conversion, add tax, shipping, duties, membership terms, and possible return costs.

When history is insufficient

If there is only one price point, the source has expired, or product identity cannot be established, the correct conclusion is that there is not enough history. Do not infer a downward trend or a true all-time low.
